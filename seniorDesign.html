Class/Project Overview

In Senior Design, I developed skills in prototyping, iterating, and principle-based design. I worked with a group to help out a local business with a problem they were facing - in this case, art school professor Dr. Mary Ruppert-Stroescu. Dr. Ruppert-Stroescu runs a fashion company that makes clothing out of strips and odd shapes of recycled fabrics. To do this, she uses a water-soluble sheeting that the scraps are sewn into. Our project was to create a washing machine that could remove the sheeting efficiently without causing any warping to the clothing items.

Our project aims to work with WashU’s own Dr. Ruppert-Stroescu and her organization RECLEM to promote textile repurposing and sustainable clothes design. As we learned in our interview, Reclem’s original process of repurposing textiles is incredibly complicated and arduous. One step in particular, washing the resewn clothing, has proved especially difficult. To keep the textile patterns in order, Reclem glues a water-soluble synthetic sheet to the clothing. Once the design and patterning of the item is complete, the sheet is washed off. An ongoing challenge for Dr. Ruppert-Stroescu has been to wash the clothing in a way where the plastic film is not only completely dissolved, but the clothing is also not damaged or deformed. Thus far, a reliable solution has not been found. This is the premise for our senior design project. We plan to design and build a machine that is able to effectively wash the repurposed clothing without warping the fabric.
